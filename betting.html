What do spread / moneyline / total mean?
Moneyline is a bet on who simply wins the game. A negative number is the favorite -- e.g. −148 means you'd bet $148 to win $100 (plus your $148 back). A positive number is the underdog -- e.g. +124 means a $100 bet wins $124. Spread handicaps the favorite by a point margin so both sides are roughly equally likely to "cover" -- the favorite (shown negative, e.g. KC −3.0) must win by more than 3 to cover; the underdog (shown positive, e.g. DEN +3.0) covers by losing by less than 3, or winning outright. Total (over/under) is a bet on the combined score of both teams, over or under one number. Each Model pick below is the side of the spread our model favors: if the model's projected margin beats the spread, it takes the favorite; if it falls short, it takes the underdog with the points. When the two match exactly, there's no lean.
Accountability

Track Record

Every game is graded two ways: did the spread pick cover, and did the team the model projected to win actually win? They can be different teams -- the model can project KC to win by 1.5 yet pick DEN +3, because 1.5 falls short of KC's 3-point spread.

Educational research project, not betting advice. Picks come from one tunable model's output, not a guarantee of results, and this is not a recommendation to wager real money.

Football Forecast is a living project -- models and methods get refined as the season teaches us things, and past results may be re-graded when a fix changes how they should be scored. What you see always reflects the current method.
